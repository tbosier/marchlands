# Trade and geographic worlds — September 21, 2026

This implements the first playable slice of the
[base-game direction](BASE_GAME_DIRECTION.md). It adds journeys and geographic
decisions without adding another upgrade tree.

## Playing it

Open **World**, choose a size and seed, then **Create world**. Save the current
march first. New worlds have rivers, forest stands, ridges and passes; the keep
starts in a fertile clearing and the rival town remains within useful early
trading distance. The default opening and older saves retain legacy geography.

| Preset | Side length | Area relative to Small |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Small | 768 m | 1× |
| Medium | 1,536 m | 4× |
| Large | 3,072 m | 16× |
| Extra large | 6,144 m | 64× |

Build a **Market** and open **Trade**. A civilian becomes the actual merchant:
their name, home, injuries and identity persist, while local production loses
one worker. Soldiers and merchants share the same finite population. Recalling
a caravan orders a physical return; the person resumes civilian work at home.

The ordinary offer is **24 timber for 8 iron**. A loner offers **12 for 4**, with
one outstanding promise at a time. Other personalities trade while access is
open; war closes trade. The neighbor starts with 32 iron and does not replenish
it. Repeat trips stop and explain why when stock or access runs out.

Dispatch reserves goods. The merchant walks to collect food and timber, visits
the market, travels to the neighbor, exchanges goods there, and brings imports
home. Cargo and provisions share the cart's 48-unit capacity. The offer displays
travel food and estimated round-trip time; dispatch preserves two days of food
at home. Travel consumes food, and actual movement wears the route.

Interrupted offers release their promises. Destroyed stores, missing routes,
depleted provisions and war have explicit return/waiting behavior. A dead
merchant remains dead; the cart's remaining cargo stays at the incident site.
**Recover goods** assigns real civilians to collect and carry it home. Canceling
recovery preserves cargo already in a worker's hands.

## Timber crossings

Open **Bridge**, click one bank and hover the other. The preview shows validity,
span, materials and estimated walking detour saved. Click again to commission
construction. Right-click or Escape cancels placement.

- Spans are 8–64 m, four metres wide, with a maximum 18% deck slope.
- Timber cost is `ceil(12 + 1.5 × span)`; tools cost `ceil(1 + span / 16)`.
- Work takes `12 + 3 × span` worker-seconds after physical material deliveries.
- No research is required. The unfinished site provides no crossing.
- Completed decks carry people and carts above the water and affect routing.
- Removing an occupied bridge is refused. Destruction evacuates occupants to
  a bank and invalidates routes. Canceling construction refunds delivered goods
  when storage permits; materials already carried by workers stay with them.

## Persistence and verification

Saves record dimensions and generation version, caravan phases and identities,
stock promises, cargo, bridge progress, wrecks and recovery orders. Older saves
keep their original 768 m geography. Invalid replacements leave the live march
intact. Tests compare loaded state against an independent live-state snapshot.

The new suites cover:

- Legacy terrain compatibility and all four world sizes, including distant
  routing, fertile starts, river crossings and local wear updates.
- Real paid construction, deck movement, cancellation and destruction.
- Finite trade, every travel/save phase, repeat exhaustion, interruptions,
  citizen conservation, permanent death and physical salvage.
- A full simulation that builds a useful crossing, sends a merchant across it,
  saves on the deck, restores and receives the imports at home.
- Actual UI clicks for world selection, trade and two-bank placement at several
  window sizes; screenshots in `artifacts/connections_ui/`.

Run `tools/build.sh test` for the complete gate, including existing long-run
settlement and military coverage. The September 21 run passed **41/41 stages**:
`artifacts/verification/20260921-132409-398137/summary.json`.
This includes three 90-day established settlements, three 120-day paid openings,
all new headless suites, and actual rendered input through the new controls.
The lost-cart recovery buttons and alert cleanup also pass the rendered test.

A final save-validation rerun after review passed all 129 rejected fixtures and
new positive cases for saves during enemy attacks and serving soldiers beyond
the legacy map boundary: `artifacts/save_validation_final.log`. Review also
fixed merchant identity-counter drift and integer seed-bound validation.

## Measured scale and limits

On the development machine, standalone XL generation took about 17 seconds
headless, a distant route about 182 ms, and peak process memory about 427 MB.
Generating, saving and restoring a full XL opening took about a minute in the
combined integration run. These are observations, not hardware requirements.

A stationary XL terrain/resource scene on the RTX 5090 using Godot's OpenGL
Compatibility renderer measured 0.864 ms median frame interval and 4.535 ms p95.
This excludes settlement simulation and is not a full-game frame-rate claim.
The same terrain is much slower under software rendering. Reproduction scripts:
`game/tests/world_sizes.gd` and `game/tests/world_size_render.gd`.

This remains one neighboring town and one finite barter offer. There is no
price market, export replenishment industry, automatic escort assignment,
caravan waypoint editor or scouting system. Larger worlds add geography and
space, not additional civilizations. Long-distance pacing and long-session
balance need player testing. Those are the next base-game questions, rather
than reasons to add more upgrade tiers now.
