# Production, meals and asset follow-up — 18 September 2026

This follows [the earlier verification pass](VERIFICATION_2026-09-18.md).
It addresses production at full stores, meal delays, the untested paid tools
chain, and the three remaining asset warnings.

## Production and deliveries

A logging camp with one free storage unit could post three gathering trips
carrying up to 36 units. Once the other stores filled, the returning workers
had nowhere to put the excess. Gathering and harvesting jobs now reserve room
before they are posted. Deposits respect both incoming hauls and production
claims; completion or retirement releases the owning claim exactly once.
Farm claims allow for the crop reaching full ripeness during the trip.

The focused regression checks full stores, resumed production after spending,
worker abandonment, job retirement, farm demolition and competing deposits.
The endurance tests compare every building's claims with its live jobs.

A separate workshop bug sent inputs from a smith to itself when its stock was
below one crafting batch. Source selection now excludes the receiving workshop.
The regression proves that an external store supplies the missing input.

## Meals and remote work

A late breakfast immediately before the next scheduled meal could leave a
citizen due to eat again within seconds. A meal now provides at least the
shortest regular meal interval. Ordinary breakfast and dinner times still work.
Jobless carriers eat when a meal is due, and loaded workers allow time to reach
food before hunger becomes urgent. Their cargo and delivery claims survive
the break. Due meals take precedence over sleep.

Travel estimates use actual walking speed, including surfaces, roads and cargo.
They are cached rather than recomputed every tick. Empty outbound workers
retain the opportunity to reach their workplace; an early draft interrupted
remote miners so often that they never extracted ore. Paid industry checks
caught that regression before integration.

The first full integration pass then caught the same risk on loaded outbound
construction deliveries: seed 42's replacement mine waited from day 37 to 120
for timber held by two workers repeatedly returning for meals. The final rule
also checks the destination travel budget before interrupting a delivery.
The bootstrap retains its late-crafting assertion and now explicitly rejects
paid construction stalled for fifteen days at the test horizon. That added
assertion failed against the earlier code at day 60.

With the final delivery rule and corrected assets imported, the same 90-day
established-settlement fixture produced these results:

## Generator corrections

The granary, keep and cart generators now center their complete horizontal
bounds, move attachment points with the geometry, and round placement bounds
outward. The exporter and validation tolerances were not relaxed.

| Asset | Corrected footprint | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Granary | 7.3 × 11.2 m | Include the stairs and loading platform |
| Keep | 16.5 × 20 m | Center the complete structure and stairs |
| Cart | 2.4 × 3.3 m | Center wheels and shafts together |

Cart cargo now uses the generated stock attachment, so it stays in the bed
after recentering. All 28 generated assets validate without warnings.
Repeat generation produced identical GLBs and manifests. All three turntable
previews and the granary icon were regenerated and inspected. Saved building
centers remain unchanged; existing saves render these assets with their new
local offsets and larger corrected placement bounds.

## Paid industry coverage

The new bootstrap fixture starts with the normal opening settlement and pays
for logging, two farms, a quarry, granary, mine, blacksmith, stockpile, housing
and a forge upgrade. Builders haul the real costs; no production building or
resource stock is gifted. It runs for 120 days on each of the three seeds.

Ore is finite. The fixture pays for replacement mines at real deposits when
available, records global exhaustion, and checks that the settlement maintains
its late tool supply. This exercises one opening strategy on three maps;
it does not establish balance for every layout or an indefinitely renewable
mineral economy.

Seeds 1776 and 42 made another 112 and 88 tools after day 60. Production totals
are inferred from final stock plus measured consumption, so the table rounds
small floating-point residuals. Seed 20260911 had no remaining ore and maintained
its bonus from previously crafted tools. All planned buildings and replacements
completed through normal construction, with no self-hauls or reservation errors.

The more distant industrial layouts still had daily urgent-hunger observations
on 14, 29 and 45 of 120 days respectively. None experienced famine or late tool
shortages. Loaded citizens without a route were observed transiently on 0, 5
and 6 days; none remained in that state at the horizon. These metrics are
observations, not guarantees that every outpost layout avoids delays. Full
stores can legitimately pause production, and finite ore still limits how
long a settlement can replenish tools.
