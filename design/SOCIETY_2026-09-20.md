# Citizens, cattle and armor — September 20, 2026

This extends the market/frontier slice with finite manpower and a physical
livestock-to-equipment chain. Existing roads, markets and supply rules still
apply.

## People and service

Recruitment transfers an existing settled citizen into military service. Their
name, age, home, hunger, carried goods and identity remain theirs. With 20
residents, recruiting 10 leaves 10 civilians and 10 soldiers; recruiting all 20
leaves no production workforce. Soldiers still occupy their homes and count
against housing and food demand. Recruitment cannot manufacture citizens.

A completed barracks is required. Recruitment costs five tools and up to ten
food; returning veterans receive credit for unused personal rations. Units
start where their citizen was standing. Their former jobs and reservations are
released immediately, including carts and unfinished production.

Select a friendly soldier and choose Return to civilian life to discharge them.
Discharge is blocked during war when a living enemy is within 24 metres. The
returning person keeps injuries, personal food, equipment and carried goods.
Their work capacity reflects injuries; a veteran without usable arms cannot
produce or build. Reenlisting does not heal injuries. Death permanently removes
the person and frees their housing place.

The rival's reinforcements also draw from its finite physical growers. Enlisting
them reduces its harvest capacity; exhausting that workforce stops new harvests
and further recruitment. Its wider town population remains simplified.

## Located injuries and equipment

Hits target the head, torso, either arm or either leg. Slash, stab and blunt
impacts produce cuts, punctures and bruises with different armor resistance.
Severe penetrating slashes can sever limbs; stabs cannot amputate. Missing or
disabled sword arms prevent sword and firepot attacks, and leg injuries reduce
movement. Fatal head/torso trauma and accumulated injuries can kill a unit.

The model has discrete body regions rather than anatomical physics. Limbs and
weapons visibly disappear or drop, wounds mark the body, and armor has distinct
geometry. There are no soldier health bars. The inspection panel describes
injuries. Wounds currently persist: medicine, prosthetics and recovery are not
implemented.

Armor protects its covered regions. Leather provides light protection; mail
resists cuts; plate includes mail underneath. A moderate chest stab against
plate over mail can become a bruise. Equipment requires both research and an
individual material payment within 24 metres of a reachable completed barracks.
Switching equipment does not refund the old suit.

## Discovering ranching

Build and staff a Cattle Ranch, then use Wild cattle to locate a herd. Select a
wild cow and request domestication. A rancher must walk to the animal, tame it
and lead it home; clicking does not teleport it or grant resources. The first
successful delivery discovers Ranching. Completing a market and paying for the
study unlocks breeding and surplus slaughter.

A ranch employs two citizens, holds eight cattle, and can receive wild cattle
within 240 metres. Tending two adults accumulates breeding work; calves mature
at six days. Slaughter keeps at least three adults and consumes one surplus
animal for 20 food and four hides. Meat and hides are physically hauled out;
output space is reserved so competing work cannot overfill the yard. Unstaffed
ranches do not breed or slaughter.

Leatherworking enables the Tannery. Each leather consumes one hide and a
quarter unit of timber. Leather armor, mail and plate then compete for actual
unreserved materials with the rest of the settlement.

| Study | Prerequisite | Materials | Days |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Ranching | First cow brought home | 12 timber, 15 food | 2 |
| Leatherworking | Ranching | 15 timber, 4 hides | 2 |
| Mail armor | Leatherworking | 20 iron, 4 leather, 5 tools | 3 |
| Plate armor | Mail armor | 35 iron, 6 leather, 10 tools | 4 |

All studies require a completed market. Material costs are additional to the
cost of each suit and building.

domestication orders, breeding work and discoveries. Old five-resource
